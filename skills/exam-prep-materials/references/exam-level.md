# Pitching material at the right level

Past exams are often easier than the current instructor's latest one. After a
hard midterm the student asked to be taught "as if preparing for exams of this
difficulty" from then on. Calibrate everything (practice sets, problem books,
explanations, cheat-sheet updates) to the latest real exam.

## How to calibrate

1. Get the latest real exam (photos after the exam; see
   `answer-keys-and-exams.md`) and write its key.
2. Sort its parts into **standard** (a derivation or result from the notes,
   asked directly) and **differentiators** (parts the instructor flags as
   challenging, or that need a move the notes never show). In one exam about
   70% was standard, 20–30% differentiators.
3. If the student shares their score per part, see where the points were lost.
   The usual picture: most points kept on the standard parts, most of the
   differentiators lost. Then the plan is: make the standard proofs airtight
   first, then train the differentiating moves explicitly.

## The three moves that separated the grades

1. **An unfamiliar system given only through an equation of state.** For
   example the pressure as a function of temperature and chemical potential,
   P(T, μ), for a medium never seen in class. The move: recognise it as a
   fundamental relation (via the Gibbs–Duhem relation P(T, μ) determines
   everything), derive S, N, U from it, then change variables.
2. **"To leading (nonzero) order in a small parameter".** A perturbative
   inversion: expand, invert the series to the order asked, and stop there;
   say which order is kept and why the next one is smaller. Near a critical
   point, "to leading order in T_c − T" (for example a coexistence width ∝
   √(T_c − T)).
3. **A generalisation that breaks a symmetry the standard example relies
   on.** The textbook model is symmetric, so its critical point sits at the
   symmetric value; the exam's version is asymmetric, the shortcut fails, and
   the general conditions are needed (for a critical point: the second and
   third derivatives vanish together).

## In practice

- Every practice set includes at least one problem of each kind.
- When teaching a standard result, also show its twisted version: the
  asymmetric model, the system known only through an unusual equation of
  state, the next order.
- Keep the standard derivations airtight: a missing step in a standard proof
  costs as many points as a hard part.
